Solving for x

Now that we have a rigid body and we have a description of all the forces acting on that body, the next step is calculating the actual changes to position. The issue is that this is a Hard Problem to solve explicitly.

In fact, these problems (depending on your forces) are likely “analytically intractable”, meaning that calculating an exact solution is not possible. This is because what we’re looking at is called an Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE). You can’t just multiply through from forces described in terms of acceleration to get position. Instead, the general approach is to iteratively calculate an approximation to the explicit solution.

The blue line is the explicit solution for some trajectory. The red, segmented line is the iterative approximation to it, calculated at small “steps” in time, called dt, like 1/60th of a second (here dt=0.033 s). Most methods, in most cases, will be able to stay closer to the explicit solution with a smaller dt. However, more steps lead to more time spent processing.

Now you can see one of the tensions inside of a physics engine. We want to stay “close enough” to that blue line so that our physics engine doesn’t explode. At the same time, we’d like to spend the least possible amount of computation doing it.

Scrub dt and the red polyline hugs or leaves the blue curve; touch the curve to step back down to one instant.
