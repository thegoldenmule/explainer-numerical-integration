ODEs vs PDEs; intractability

An ordinary differential equation has one independent variable. Ours is time: the state of the point mass is a few numbers, and the equation says how they change as t advances.

Add a second independent variable and you have a partial differential equation. A vibrating string or a heat bar next to the point mass has a value at every point in space as well as every instant in time, so its “state” is a whole function, and the equation couples the change in time to the shape in space:

∂2u∂t2 =c2 ∂2u∂x2

That is a different problem, with boundary conditions and a grid of unknowns per step. Rigid body engines stay with ODEs.

Even the ODE is generally intractable. The linear spring has a closed form (the blue curve), but add a nonlinear force and the closed form goes away, and an engine has many bodies with many forces each. Why can’t we just figure out the exact solution to a complex system? Among other reasons, see Bonini’s paradox: a model complete enough to be exact is as hard to understand as the thing it models.

Poke the string and the point mass side by side; one is a number over time, the other is a shape over time.
