Taylor expansion and 1/r² gravity

Any other forces (like perhaps magnetism or lift on an airfoil) can be linearized by using something called a Taylor series expansion. This creates an infinite series, from which we can take the lower-order terms and then simply discard the non-linear terms.

Around an operating distance r0, with δ=r−r0:

1r2= 1r02 −2δr03 +3δ2r04 −…

Keep the first term and gravity is a constant, mg. Keep two and it is linear in δ. Everything after that is what the linear model throws away, and it is small only while δ is small.

Then the payoff: run the nonlinear simulation next to the linearized stability verdict. Far from r0 you can find cases where the prediction says stable and the simulation explodes. Those are the details that have fallen through the cracks of the abstraction.

Slide the number of terms kept and watch the series close on the real curve; then push the mass away from r0 until the verdict and the simulation disagree.
