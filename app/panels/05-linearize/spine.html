Stability analysis: linearize

Like the name implies, LST requires that our system of equations is linear. This means we can’t have any squared or higher power terms. Notice the squared term under Newton’s gravitation, Gm1m2/r2? That makes this thing non-linear, which is bad news for LST. This is why physics engines generally do not use the “real” Newtonian model. Instead, we simplify to a linear model that doesn’t divide by distance at all: gravity is a constant g times mass, drag is proportional to velocity, and a spring is proportional to the distance it is stretched. Anything else is linearized with a Taylor series expansion.

Any combination of those models will sum to something that looks like this:

1·x″ +0.1·x′ +100·x=0

What we’re left with is a description of force that is proportional to position, velocity, and acceleration. M, C, and K in Mx″+Cx′+Kx=0 are constants derived from our specific force parameters; from here on they are the m, c, k you drag. There is also a constant term, but we can discard that as it will not affect stability.

Every number in a force’s equation is draggable, and each force has a switch between its real model and its linear one; watch which parameters survive into the coefficients above.
