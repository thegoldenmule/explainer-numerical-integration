Explode h: nested disks

Since our time step h is in the inequality, it heavily affects the stability of a method. Before, the disk was drawn for one h; here it is drawn for a small range of step sizes at once, all on one plane, with the eigenvalues fixed.

Each disk has radius 1/h and is centered at −1/h, so every one of them touches the origin from the left and none of them reaches the right half plane. Halving the step doubles the disk: at 1/30 s the radius is 30, at 1/60 s it is 60. With a 1/60 second step the region of stability scales, so it does include eigenvalues for which it would previously have been unstable.

But the disks are nested, not growing in every direction. Near the imaginary axis the edge is nearly tangent to it, so a lightly damped spring, whose eigenvalues sit just left of that axis and far up it, needs a very small h to fall inside, and an undamped one never does. Shrinking h buys time; it does not buy oscillation.
