Local vs global error

Local truncation error is the error of one step, starting from the exact state. For Euler it is the first term the method dropped from the Taylor series,

h22x″+… (RK4: h5120x(5)+…)

Global error is what has accumulated at time t after t/h of those steps, each one starting from the previous step’s already-wrong state. Roughly, the count multiplies away one power of h: Euler’s local h2 becomes a global h, and RK4’s local h5 becomes a global h4. That exponent is what “fourth order” means.

The two are not the same thing, and stability is a third. Local error says how wrong one step is; the ratio from panel 9 says whether those errors compound. A controller can only see the first, one step at a time, and that is what it steers by.

Take one step of h=0.033 s from the exact curve and read its local error; then let the steps run and watch the global error pull away from the local one.
