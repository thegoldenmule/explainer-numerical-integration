Variable step sizes

Conventional wisdom says that you just pick the method with the largest stability region. However, not only does this not guarantee stability (you still need to do the work of calculating your eigenvalues), it may be that we are instead over-calculating. Ideally we want to be able to make h as large as possible without blowing up our simulation. This is where local truncation error comes in.

Euler and RK4 are both the first few terms of an infinite Taylor series. This is good news, because it means we can simply take the next term in the Taylor expansion as our error estimate: for Euler that is the h2 term, for RK4 the h5 term. These are both easily calculable in real-time. What this allows us to do is not just measure error, but work backward and instead describe a target error threshold.

For example, we could specify a target local truncation error of 0.01. If local error is less than 0.01, then we can increase h until we hit that bound. If it is greater, we can either lower h or just keep adding expansion terms. In college I implemented such a system:

Using the error bound of 0.01 for both, I found that the Euler method worked well in most situations but the RK4 method was so much more accurate that [the engine] attempted to set the step size as great as 16 seconds! This resulted in a frame rate of 1/16 frames per second.
