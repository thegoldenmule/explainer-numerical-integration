Adaptive step controllers

The control loop, step by step: take a step, estimate its local error, compare it with the target, and set the next step from the ratio. For a method of order p the local error scales like hp+1, so the step that would just meet the target is

hnext=h· (targeterror)1/(p+1)

usually with a safety factor and a cap on how fast h may grow. If the error came in over the target, the step is rejected and retried smaller. The estimate comes from the next Taylor term, or from taking the step twice at half size and comparing.

Then explode the target. A sweep of target errors produces a small bundle of h(t) profiles: loose targets race to large steps and stay there while the spring is quiet; tight targets crawl. This is where the cost-versus-accuracy tradeoff from panels 3 and 8 finally becomes a dial.

A local-error tolerance never enforces stability: adaptive Euler on the demo spring is slowed, not saved.
