Point masses

There are loads of physics engines out there that you may not realize are physics engines. Most particle systems are one. They don’t generally care about fine-grain collision detection, and they treat each physical object as a dimension-less point. That point is where we start.

A point mass is all the mass at one point: no extent, no rotation, no near side and far side, nothing to spin. Its entire state is a position on the plane and a number,

(x,y) = (1.00,0.50) , m=1.00

Drag it anywhere you like. Nothing else moves: there are no forces yet, so the position is whatever you last set it to, and the mass only starts to matter once something pushes on it. Every panel after this one is about what happens next to exactly this object.

A real body has dimensions, and a body with dimensions can also turn. That is the step up, to the right: the rigid body, with a rotation θ alongside its position. This piece drops θ and keeps the point.
