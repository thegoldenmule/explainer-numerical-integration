Frame budget, ms per frame

A display refreshing 60 times a second gives each frame 1/60 s≈16.7 ms. At 30 fps it is 33.3 ms. A millisecond is a thousandth of a second; a modern CPU does a few million simple operations in one.

Here is a single frame as a timeline. Everything the game does that frame has to fit: reading input, running the physics step, updating the world, drawing it. The physics slice is what this piece is about. If the slice grows past the frame, the frame is late and the display shows the previous one again: a stutter.

The physics step and the frame are different clocks. The step size h is how much simulated time one step advances; the slice is how much wall-clock time computing it costs. Advancing simulated time by one frame at a small h takes several steps, and several slices. That is why a larger step is cheaper, and why panel 8 wants the largest one that stays close.

Drag h and count the steps that fit inside the one frame.
