Explode h: all step sizes at once

The spine runs one step size. Here the same simulation runs across a small spread of step sizes at once, one trajectory per h, all against the same exact curve.

Read it as a bundle. The small steps lie on the blue curve and cost the most; the large steps peel away first, and some never come back. Between them is the step this spring can afford. The spine’s single h is one line in this bundle, and the trial-and-error developer from the spine is walking this range one guess at a time.

Two numbers for the highlighted run are printed in the corner of the chart: its compute cost, the steps it takes per second of simulated time, and its error, how far it has drifted from the exact solution. As h grows, one falls and the other rises, and the panels after this one are about proving where that trade stops being safe.

The window is a few periods of the current spring and the vertical range is the exact curve’s own amplitude, so the bundle oscillates at a readable scale and a run that peels away simply leaves the frame.

Drag the h marker along the range to highlight the trajectory it lands on and dim the rest.
