Under-, critically, and overdamped

Sweep the damping and the discriminant c2−4mk passes through zero. The two roots slide along the real axis, collide at −c/2m, and split up and down into the plane. Beside them the closed-form solution changes shape.

Overdamped, c2>4mk: two real roots r1, r2, and the mass creeps home without crossing zero, x=Aer1t+Ber2t.

Critically damped, c2=4mk: one repeated root, the fastest return with no overshoot.

Underdamped, c2<4mk: a conjugate pair α±iω, and the mass rings inside a decaying envelope:

x=eαt (x0cosωt+ v0−αx0ω sinωt)

with α=−c/2m. The real part of λ is the decay rate; the imaginary part is the ring frequency. Both games and springs live here: the demo spring has c=0.1 against a critical 2mk=20.

Drag c across the critical value and watch the roots collide and the curve stop ringing.
