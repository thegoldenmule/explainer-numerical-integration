Newtonian physics: F = ma

When we talk about “rigid body physics engines”, we are generally talking about engines that use Newtonian physics. Even more specifically, what we’re talking about is Newton’s second law. This law lets us describe forces based on mass m and acceleration a, the latter being the second derivative of position x.

F=ma=mx″

The high-level approach of rigid body physics engines is to describe all the forces acting on a rigid body and sum them together. Here we have wind pushing on the object, drag pulling it another direction, and gravity pulling the object down. We can sum all of these to get the total of all our forces.

∑iFi=mx″

Each of these individual forces is calculated based on a model for that specific force. The wind force is probably just a constant vector. It’s always windy, everywhere, in the same direction. Newton’s gravitational force model is an attractive force based on the mass of both objects, divided by their distance squared. We’ll revisit that one.

Each force beside the plane is its model, written out, and every number in it is draggable. Drag one and the arrow it produces changes on the plane; drag an arrow’s head instead and the number changes. Toggle each force on and off, drag the mass itself, and step time once by hand; ∑iFi and the resulting a follow.
