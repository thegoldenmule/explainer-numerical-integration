Explode the eigenvector over a sweep

The spine shows one matrix and its invariant directions. Now sweep the matrix across a range of one transformation and draw the eigenvectors for every value at once.

Along a scale sweep the picture is dull: the two eigenvectors stay where they are and only their eigenvalues change. Along a rotation sweep something happens. Start from a pure stretch, with two eigenvectors at right angles, and add rotation. The two invariant directions lean toward each other, meet, and then vanish: past that angle no real arrow comes back pointing the way it went.

They have not disappeared, they have gone complex. A rotation by θ has eigenvalues

λ=cosθ±i·sinθ

and a complex eigenvalue is exactly what a matrix has when it turns everything and holds nothing fixed. Its modulus is still a scale factor; its angle is a turn per application. That is the bridge to the complex plane, where the next panel puts the spring’s own eigenvalues and they stay for the rest of the piece.

Mouse over a value in the range to highlight its eigenvectors and dim the rest; find the angle where they meet.
