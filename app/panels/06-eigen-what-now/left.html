Matrices as transforms

A matrix is a rule for moving every point of the plane at once. Give the point a third coordinate pinned to 1 and a single 3×3 matrix carries the whole transformation — it skews, it rotates, it translates:

M=[ scosθ−ssinθtx ssinθscosθty 001 ]

The bottom row is never anything but [001]: bookkeeping that keeps the third coordinate at 1. The first two columns are where the two unit arrows land; the third column is where the origin lands.

Three transforms drive it — translate, rotate, scale, composing as T(0.00,0.00) ·R(0°) ·S(1.00) — and the matrix is the output. Drag one and the entries it writes light up: translate owns the third column, rotate owns the whole top-left block, and scale owns the two diagonal entries, until you have turned the shape, after which rotate and scale share all four, because s multiplies every one of them.

Grid lines stay straight and parallel under all of it. No eigenvectors, no invariant directions yet: matrices as transformations, nothing more.
