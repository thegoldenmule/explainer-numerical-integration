What “close” means

Take exactly two curves: the exact solution φ(t) and one neighbor ψ(t) that started a little way off. “Close” is one number, their distance at each instant:

d(t)=|ψ(t)−φ(t)|

“Starts close” means d(0) is small. “Stays close” means d(t) never grows past some bound, no matter how long you wait. Plotted over time, that is a line that either flattens out or climbs.

For the spring the distance is itself a solution of the same equation, because the equation is linear: the difference of two solutions is a solution. So whether neighbors stay close does not depend on which neighbor you picked, or how far off it started. It depends only on the system. That is what the next panels compute.

Drag the neighbor’s starting point and watch the distance line: stable systems flatten it, unstable ones send it climbing.
