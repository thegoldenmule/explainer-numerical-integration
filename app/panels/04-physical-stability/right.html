Lyapunov vs asymptotic

Sweep the starting state over the whole plane of (x0,v0) and each point becomes an arrow: where the system carries that state next. The picture is a phase portrait, and “stable” comes in three flavors, named for Lyapunov.

Stable (in Lyapunov’s sense): for any tube of radius ε there is a starting distance δ such that neighbors starting within δ stay within ε. The undamped spring, c=0, is this: orbits circle the origin forever, never nearer and never farther.

Asymptotically stable: stable, and the neighbors also converge to the solution. The damped spring spirals in.

Unstable: some neighbor leaves, however close it started. Negative damping spirals out.

The essay’s definition is the first of these, and the eigenvalue test in panel 7 gives the second: a strictly negative real part is asymptotic; a zero real part is the borderline circling case.

The three portraits are side by side. Drag c through zero and watch the spiral reverse.
