Taylor series

Euler and RK4 belong to the same family: they are both the first few terms of an infinite Taylor series. The exact modal step multiplies by ehλ, and the exponential expands as

ez=1+z +z22! +z33! +z44! +…

Cut it after two terms and you have Euler’s 1+hλ. Cut it after five and you have RK4’s q(hλ). Each extra term matches one more derivative of the true curve at the start of the step.

For the current spring at h=0.033 the exact factor has modulus 0.9983. Two terms give 1.0525, too big, and the error grows; five give 0.9983, and it does not. The polynomial only has to be close where hλ actually is, and a small step keeps it close to zero, where every polynomial agrees with the exponential.

Add terms to the expansion one at a time and watch the approximation of ehλ improve. Wherever the strip is set, its n terms give |Sn(hλ)|=0.9983 against the same exact 0.9983, and it does not grow.
