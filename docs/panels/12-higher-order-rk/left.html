Taylor series

Euler and RK4 belong to the same family: they are both the first few terms of an infinite Taylor series. The exact modal step multiplies by ehλ, and the exponential expands as

ez=1+z +z22! +z33! +z44! +…

Truncate it after two terms and you have Euler’s 1+hλ. Truncate it after five terms and you have RK4’s q(hλ). Each extra term matches one more derivative of the true curve at the start of the step.

For the current spring at h=0.033 the exact factor has modulus 0.9983. Two terms give 1.0525, which is too big and thus the error grows. Five terms gives 0.9983, and the error will now shrink. The polynomial only has to be close where hλ actually is.

Add terms to the expansion one at a time and watch the approximation of ehλ improve. Wherever the strip is set, its n terms give |Sn(hλ)|=0.9983 against the same exact 0.9983, and it does not grow.
