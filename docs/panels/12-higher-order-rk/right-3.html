Verlet

A different structure. Störmer–Verlet does not carry a velocity at all; it updates position from the two previous positions:

xi+1=2xi−xi−1+h2ai

With a velocity-dependent force like drag, the velocity it needs is the one it has not computed yet, so it is solved implicitly or lagged as (xi−xi−1)/h. The method is symplectic: it preserves amplitude and drifts phase, instead of decaying like implicit Euler or exploding like explicit Euler. That is why particle systems and cloth use it.

It has no scalar R(hλ), so there is no region to shade on the λ plane. The honest picture is the spectral radius ρ of its 2×2 update, drawn as a heatmap over the step per natural period, hω, and the damping ratio ζ. The wall at hω=2 is a hard edge: for the demo spring, ω=10, it is stable at h=0.19 (ρ=0.99) and unstable at h=0.20 (ρ=1.21).

Three ways out of the Euler disk, then: more terms, solve backward, or change the structure. Hover a cell of the heatmap to see its trajectory against the exact curve.
