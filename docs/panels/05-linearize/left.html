What “linear” means

A function is linear when the output of a sum is the sum of the outputs, and scaling the input scales the output by the same amount:

f(a+b)=f(a)+f(b) f(sa)=s·f(a)

A spring passes: f(x)=kx, so stretching it twice as far pulls twice as hard, and two stretches add. So does drag, cv. A squared term fails the same check:

(a+b)2≠ a2+b2 , because (a+b)2= a2+2ab+b2

That cross term is what makes 1/r2 gravity non-linear, and it is why the sum of forces can collapse to three coefficients only after every force has been made linear. A sum of linear functions is linear; add one squared term and the whole sum stops being so.
